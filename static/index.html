His own work

Local inference on the 3090

A coding agent that runs on his own desk. A 27-billion-parameter model on one 24 GB card, driving an agent harness, measured before it was trusted: 43 tokens a second at 55K context, fast enough to work with. Speculative decoding bought the last 44% (30.0 to 43.2). Without it the card holds 144K tokens of context, at 23.2 GiB.

  • The principle of least action: Give every path one number, the action S = ∫(T − V) dt. Nature takes the path where small detours don't change it: δS = 0. That single condition gives the Euler–Lagrange equation, and from it F = ma. A Legendre transform, H = pq̇ − L, trades velocity for momentum, and Hamilton's equations follow. Feynman's reason: every path counts, but only near δS = 0 do they add up instead of cancelling. The camera that flew you in rode such a path, like a bead on a wire. After Erik Verlinde's classical mechanics lectures (UvA, c. 2009). A year later he asked where F = ma itself comes from.
  • A drop on the glass: Watch a drop run down the glass. No one can predict which way it turns next. Science calls that chaos, as if it were out of control. David Abram puts it better: not out of control, only out of ours. Wild.
