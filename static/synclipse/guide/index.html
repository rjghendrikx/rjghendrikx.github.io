Where do I change the audio delay?

Lips and voice not together? First find out which way: is the sound early or late? Then change the delay on one device. Below, per kind of device and per brand, where the setting is.

Early or late? That decides everything

Most TVs, soundbars and receivers can only delay sound. That fixes sound that is early. Sound that is late gets worse when you add delay, and no slider makes it earlier.

By eye, people often disagree even about the direction. Synclipse tells you: film anyone talking on your TV with your iPhone, and it says whether the sound is early or late, by how much, and what to change. Free, no account, nothing leaves your phone.

Which device?

Correct it in one place: your soundbar or receiver if you have one, otherwise the TV, otherwise the app.

That is the device that plays the sound. Don't stack delays: the others stay at 0.

TV

General steps

  • Look in the TV's sound settings, often under Advanced or Expert.
  • It is called Audio delay, AV sync, Lip sync or Digital output delay.
  • Most TVs can only delay sound. That fixes sound that is early, not sound that is late.
  • Often it only works for sound sent to a soundbar or receiver, not for the TV's own speakers.
  • Some TVs count in milliseconds, others in plain steps. With steps: change a little, then check again.

Settings › All Settings › Sound › Expert Settings › Digital Output Audio Delay

Only for sound sent to a soundbar or receiver by HDMI-eARC or optical. Slider to the right: higher number, more sound delay. Steps may differ per model.

Samsung says no setting fixes the delay of Bluetooth or wireless speakers.

2021 and later. With Bypass off. For the TV speakers or a Sound Share device on HDMI (eARC/ARC). Higher value: sound later, up to 60.

2020 and earlier (webOS 5.0 and earlier). Bypass on, or Bypass off and a value from 0 to 15.

Soundbar

General steps

  • Usually a button on the soundbar's remote: Sync, AV Sync or Audio Sync. Sometimes in the maker's app.
  • Most soundbars can only delay sound. That fixes sound that is early, not sound that is late.
  • Usually in milliseconds, starting at 0.

AV receiver

General steps

  • Look for Audio Delay, Lip Sync or A/V Sync in the receiver's setup or options menu.
  • Most receivers can only delay sound, usually in milliseconds. That fixes sound that is early.
  • It is kept per input. Set it on the input you are watching.
  • Many receivers also have Auto Lip Sync: the TV tells the receiver how much to delay. You can adjust on top of it.

Streaming box

General steps

  • Most streaming boxes have no audio delay of their own. Then correct it on the TV, soundbar or receiver.
  • A few do have one. Some can even make sound earlier.
  • Restarting the box can help when the sound suddenly drifts.

Your brand

No slider: your iPhone listens and Apple TV matches wireless speakers to the TV's or receiver's speakers. Only useful with wireless speakers.

Player app

General steps

  • Some player apps have their own audio delay. Look in the audio options while a film plays.
  • It often works both ways, so it can also make sound earlier.
  • Some apps keep it per film. Check it again with the next film.
  • Streaming apps such as Netflix have none. Correct it on the device that plays the sound.

Sound late and the delay is already at 0?

Most TVs and soundbars can only delay sound, not speed it up. Is the delay at 0 everywhere and is the sound still late? Then lowering the delay can't fix it. Take away what adds delay, or use an app or box that can make sound earlier.

Switching off picture options does not help here: with a faster picture, the sound falls even further behind.

Sound still late?

  • Switch off sound modes on the soundbar or TV, such as surround, night mode or clear voice. They take time.
  • Connect the soundbar with an HDMI cable, not optical or Bluetooth.
  • Use the audio delay of the app that plays the film, if it has one. Some apps and a few streaming boxes can make sound earlier.
  • Still late? Set the sound format on the TV or box to Stereo or PCM. You lose surround sound.

Check again after each change.

Sound early? Make the picture faster

When sound is early, the picture takes too long. A TV needs time to improve the picture. Switch some of that off and the picture arrives sooner.

Only for sound that is early. With sound that is late, this makes it worse.

In one go

  • Choose the picture mode Filmmaker Mode, if your TV has it. It switches off motion smoothing, sharpening and noise reduction together.
  • No Filmmaker Mode? Try the picture mode Movie or Cinema.

Or one by one

  • Motion smoothing. Every brand has its own name for it.
  • Noise reduction.
  • Sharpness, edge enhancement and other extras that make the picture sharper.
  • Dynamic contrast and colour enhancement.

Game mode

Game mode makes the picture faster too, but it changes more: how the picture looks, and on some TVs also the sound or how the TV matches picture and sound. The difference can even grow. Try it last.

Check again after each change. No better? Set it back.
