Why is the sound out of sync with the picture on my TV?

By Ruud Hendrikx, maker of Synclipse. Updated 28 November 2026.

Picture and sound each take time to get through your TV, soundbar or streaming box, and one of them takes longer. That is a lip sync problem. Which one is slower decides the fix:

  • Sound early (you hear a word before the lips move): raise the audio delay, or make the picture faster.
  • Sound late (the lips move first): set the audio delay to 0 on every device and take away what adds delay. More audio delay makes it worse.

Find out which it is before you change anything: the fix for one makes the other worse.

Did it start suddenly?

Switch the TV and the soundbar off and on again. Then update the soundbar's software. TCL gives this advice for a TCL TV with a TCL soundbar. Still out of sync? Then find out which way.

How do I know if the sound is early or late?

Early means the sound comes before the picture: you hear a word before the lips move. Late means the sound comes after the picture: the lips move first. Can't tell which? Measure it: film someone talking on your TV with your iPhone and Synclipse says whether the sound is early or late, and by about how many milliseconds (ms).

Pick a scene with one person talking, facing you. The news works best. Dubbed films and cartoons don't work.

Which way matters because most TVs and soundbars can only delay sound, not make it earlier. That fixes sound that is early. Sound that is late gets worse when you add delay.

Why is the sound early?

The picture takes too long. A TV needs time to improve the picture. LG puts it this way: depending on how long the TV takes to produce the picture, the sound may jump ahead by a fraction of a second.

Two ways to fix it. Pick one:

  • Raise the audio delay on the device that plays the sound. The audio delay is the setting that makes the sound come later; on TV menus it is also called AV sync or lip sync. Where to find it, per brand: TV, soundbar, receiver. Set it in one place only.
  • Make the picture faster by switching off picture options such as motion smoothing. See how to make the picture faster.

Why is the sound late?

Something between the source and your ears delays the sound: an audio delay above 0, a sound mode, the cable, the sound format or Bluetooth.

Work down this list. Change one thing, then check again with Synclipse. No better? Set it back.

  1. An audio delay above 0. Lower it to 0 on every device: TV, soundbar, receiver and app. Where the setting is, per brand.
  2. Sound modes on the soundbar or TV, such as surround, night mode or clear voice. They take time. Switch them off.
  3. The cable. Connect the soundbar with an HDMI cable, not optical.
  4. The sound format. Set it to Stereo or PCM on the TV or box. Sonos advises this to remove the delay. You lose surround sound.
  5. Bluetooth speakers or headphones. See Bluetooth, below.

Leave the picture options alone: with a faster picture, the sound falls even further behind.

The audio delay is at 0 and the sound is still late. Now what?

Then lowering the delay can't fix it. Take away what adds delay: switch off the sound modes, use an HDMI cable, set the sound format to Stereo or PCM. Or use an app or box that can make sound earlier.

Those are steps 2 to 4 above. Done all three and still late? Some apps and a few streaming boxes can make sound earlier; which ones is in the guide under app or box. Streaming apps such as Netflix have no audio delay setting of their own.

Does Bluetooth make the sound late?

Yes. Bluetooth always adds delay, so the sound arrives late. A few TVs and boxes have a delay setting for headphones. Samsung says about its own TVs that no setting resolves this for Bluetooth or wireless speakers. Without such a setting, use a cable instead, or listen through the TV's speakers or a soundbar.

Which TVs and boxes have the setting is in the guide under Bluetooth headphones.

Synclipse can't hear headphones, so judge this one by eye.
