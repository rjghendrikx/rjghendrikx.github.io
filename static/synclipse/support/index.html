Is the sound of your TV in sync with the picture? Synclipse films someone talking on your TV and tells you. Free, with no account and no ads.

What you need

  • An iPhone with iOS 18 or later.
  • A TV showing one person talking towards the camera: the news, a talk show, an interview. Speech without music underneath works best.
  • 15 seconds of quiet in the room while you film.

How to measure (Quick check)

The first time, tap Continue and allow the camera and microphone. Then:

  1. Find someone talking on the TV: news or a talk show, no music.
  2. Point your iPhone at the TV and tap the face to zoom in. The frame around the face turns from amber to cyan when it is big enough, and the app says "Ready. Tap Film".
  3. Tap Film and keep the face in view for 15 seconds. Stay quiet: your voice confuses the measurement.
  4. Read the result: In sync, Slightly off or Out of sync, which way the sound is off and by roughly how much, and what to change.
  5. Changed a setting? Tap Check again and film once more. Synclipse sets the new result next to the one before: closer, further off, or the same.

You can also measure a video you filmed before: Settings > Measure a video > From Photos or From Files. Your earlier results are under the clock icon.

Questions

What do "sound early" and "sound late" mean?

Sound late: you hear a word after you see the lips say it. This is the common case with soundbars, receivers and wireless speakers, because they need time to process the sound. Sound early: you hear the word before the lips move. This usually means the TV is slow with the picture, for example because of picture processing.

When does Synclipse say "In sync"?

That depends on the setting under Settings > When is it in sync?

  • Relaxed: most people don't mind sound up to 90 ms early or 185 ms late (ITU-R BT.1359).
  • Normal: most people don't notice sound up to 45 ms early or 125 ms late (ITU-R BT.1359).
  • Strict: as strict as TV broadcasters, sound up to 40 ms early or 60 ms late (EBU R37).

Past your setting but within what most people accept, the result is Slightly off. Beyond that it is Out of sync.

Why does a Quick check give a range instead of one exact number?

Lips and voice never line up exactly, even when the TV is perfect: the mouth starts moving a little before the sound comes out. Synclipse corrects for that on average. The range shows how well the parts of this recording agree with each other: at least 30 ms either way, wider when they disagree. Two things come on top of that: every speaker's mouth leads the voice by a slightly different amount, and the show itself may be slightly off before it reaches your TV (broadcasters allow up to 40 ms early and 60 ms late). That is why a second measurement, on another show, helps. If you want a more precise number, use Fine-tune.

The sound is late. What do I do?

Lower the audio delay on the TV, soundbar or TV app by the amount the result says, and tap Check again.

Already at 0? Then no setting can fix this. Most TVs and soundbars can only delay sound, not speed it up. Change the route the sound takes instead, and measure again after each change:

  • switch off the soundbar's sound modes (surround, night mode, dialogue);
  • connect the soundbar by HDMI instead of optical or Bluetooth;
  • use the audio delay in the app that plays the film, if it has one (Infuse, VLC).

The setting has different names per brand: audio delay, AV sync or lip sync. Look in the sound settings of your TV, soundbar or TV app.

The sound is early. What do I do?

Raise the audio delay (also called AV sync or lip sync) on the TV, soundbar or TV app by the amount the result says, and tap Check again.

Synclipse says "Not sure yet"

Parts of the recording gave different answers. It shows which way the sound seems to be off, but no number. Measure again before you change anything, on another show if you can.

Synclipse says "No clear result"

It does this instead of guessing, and it says nothing about your TV yet. Usually the face was too small, turned sideways, or out of view for part of the 15 seconds, or there was music or talking in the room. Zoom in on one person who faces the camera, pick speech without music, keep the face in the frame and stay quiet. Are you watching with headphones? A Quick check can't hear them. If it happens twice in a row, Synclipse offers Fine-tune with the test video instead.

Does it work with headphones?

No. Synclipse measures the sound your iPhone's microphone hears in the room, so it cannot hear sound that plays in headphones.

What is Fine-tune?

Fine-tune measures with a test video instead of a person talking. Once a second, the video shows a white flash with a click at the same moment. Synclipse films it at a high frame rate and times each flash against its click, to within a few milliseconds for that way of playing. Two separate plays of the same video can differ by 30–40 ms (the TV's frame timing and the soundbar's buffer), so measure two or three times.

  1. Do a Quick check. Under the result, tap "Want it precise? Fine-tune with the test video". The first time, a sheet with the test video opens, and from then on the camera has a Quick check | Fine-tune switch. Synclipse always starts in Quick check.
  2. Tap Save the test video and put the file where your TV can play it. (You can get back to it with Test video, next to the switch.)
  3. Play the file on your TV the way you watch films: from a USB stick, Plex or a player app such as Infuse. Don't use YouTube or AirPlay: they send the sound along a different route, and the result would measure that route instead.
  4. Aim the frame on the screen at the flash, hold still and tap Measure. It takes 7 seconds.

The result carries a FINE-TUNE badge and gives the delay in milliseconds. It includes the app that plays the video, so measure with the app or device you normally watch with.

Where are my recordings?

In the Files app, under On My iPhone > Synclipse. You can delete them there. Nothing leaves your phone; see the privacy policy.
